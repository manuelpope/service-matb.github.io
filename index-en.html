The approach

A thin software layer on top of what the company already has. It's not about replacing people. It's about removing the work that a machine can do better.

The next generation of enterprise software doesn't have to be a huge application. It can be a small layer of intelligence and automation that connects to the systems, documents, and processes the company already runs.

The company keeps running on its existing systems. On top, a layer that automates the repetitive parts.

What I don't do. I don't sell "AI" as a label. I don't ship generic chatbots. I don't replace your stack. Every system I build starts by understanding one specific process and ends with a concrete operational improvement you can measure.
Where I work

Four verticals where the problem is most visible. Companies whose operations still depend heavily on scattered information and manual work.

Logistics & operations

Professional services

Deliverables, documents, deadlines, reviews, follow-up, and reporting. Every client is a small manual project of its own.

Companies with fragmented data

Connecting sources — ERP, CRM, spreadsheets, APIs, internal databases — and turning them into a real tool to operate with.

An example

An auto-parts request, before and after. This isn't a chatbot. It's a slice of the business turned into software.

Today
  • WhatsApp
  • ↓ Employee receives the order
  • ↓ Looks up the part number
  • ↓ Checks the internal spreadsheet
  • ↓ Asks a supplier
  • ↓ Calculates price manually
  • ↓ Replies to the customer

Hours of back-and-forth. Mistakes. Customers who leave.

→
With the system
  • Structured request
  • ↓ System interprets
  • ↓ Queries the catalog
  • ↓ Queries inventory
  • ↓ Queries suppliers
  • ↓ Calculates alternatives
  • ↓ Generates the response
  • ↓ Logs the operation

Seconds. Full traceability. The person decides, doesn't execute.

Services · how I work

Short cycles. Evidence before scale. Every engagement ends with a working system and numbers to show.

01

Diagnosis

One or two sessions to map the current process, where time is lost, and what can be automated.

02

Design

Concrete proposal: what's built, what's measured, in how long. Before writing a single line of code.

03

Build

Iterative development with weekly demos. Your team watches the system grow and can correct course.

04

Deploy

Go-live with your real data, integrated into your systems, with monitoring from day one.

05

Measure

Time saved, errors removed, speed gained. Concrete numbers, not perceptions.

What kind of results to expect

  • Measurable time saved. Person-hours the team stops spending on mechanical tasks, recorded before and after.
  • Errors that disappear. Information that no longer gets typed by hand between WhatsApp, Excel, and the ERP — so it stops getting lost or wrong.
  • Information that didn't exist before. Reports, metrics, and traceability that come out of the same system that runs the work — not a parallel spreadsheet.
  • People reassigned to higher-value work. The team stops executing repetitive tasks and starts serving customers, making decisions, and handling exceptions.
